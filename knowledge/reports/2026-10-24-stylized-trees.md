## Context and adaptations
- The tutorial targets Roblox for lighting and Blender for modeling.
  Blender techniques apply as-is. Engine lighting is translated to Godot 4.
- Our constraint: trees will appear in large numbers (forests). Anything
  that costs per tree at runtime (dynamic lights, extra draw calls) must be
  replaced by something baked into the mesh or done in a shared shader.
- Everything is generated by scripts. No hand painting.
- Art direction: stylized, light assets, modern lighting does the heavy
  lifting. Flat interiors, detail at edges and transitions.

## Techniques

### 1. Foliage as clustered cards
Canopy is built from flat alpha-textured planes, not individual leaves.
Cards are duplicated, randomly rotated and scattered inside a sphere or
ellipsoid volume per cluster. A tree = trunk/branches + several clusters.
Parameters: cards_per_cluster, cluster_radius, cluster_shape (sphere,
ellipsoid), card_size_range, rotation_jitter.

### 2. Spherical normal transfer (highest priority)
Problem: flat cards get chaotic shading and ugly self-shadowing.
Fix: overwrite each foliage vertex normal so it points out from the
cluster center, so the whole cluster shades like one soft volume.
- In Blender: UV Sphere around the cluster, Data Transfer modifier,
  Face Corner Data -> Custom Normals.
- In our generator this can be computed directly without Blender:
  normal = normalize(vertex_pos - cluster_center), optionally blended with
  the card's own normal.
Parameters: normal_blend (0 = card normal, 1 = full sphere normal),
normal_center_offset (shift center up for brighter tops),
normal_sphere_scale (0.8-1.2).
Export: custom normals must survive glTF export and Godot import
(check that the importer does not recompute them).

### 3. Double-sided foliage
Godot: cull_mode = CULL_DISABLED on the foliage material. Flip backfaces
consistently at generation time so normals are coherent with technique 2.

### 4. Alpha handling for foliage textures
- Alpha bleed / padding: fill RGB outside the alpha mask with the nearest
  leaf color so mipmaps do not produce dark edges. Do this as a
  post-process step in the texture pipeline (e.g. Pillow/numpy dilation).
- Optional light Gaussian blur on the alpha mask for fluffier, softer
  canopy edges. Parameter: alpha_blur_radius.
- Godot: prefer alpha scissor (or alpha hash) over alpha blend for
  foliage, to avoid sorting issues. Consider alpha antialiasing.

### 5. Kill specular on foliage
Cards must not show sun glints. Godot: roughness = 1.0 and
specular_mode = disabled (or specular = 0) on the foliage material.

### 6. Inner canopy color instead of point lights (ADAPTED)
The tutorial places a point light tinted the foliage color inside each
canopy, plus a weak blue light underneath. Do NOT do this: hundreds of
dynamic lights in a forest are far too expensive.
Same effect, near-zero cost:
- Bake vertex colors at generation time: a gradient from a saturated
  foliage tint in the canopy interior and lower side to the base color
  at the outer top. Bottom-facing areas get a slight cool/blue tint to
  fake sky/ground bounce.
- The foliage shader multiplies or mixes base color with vertex color,
  so inner shadows read as rich green instead of dead grey.
Parameters: inner_tint_color, inner_tint_strength, bottom_cool_tint,
gradient_falloff.
Optional: Godot's backlight property on the foliage material for
light shining through leaves.

### 7. Scene lighting (Godot translation, not part of the generator)
For the test scene and contact sheet renders:
- Warm, low-angle DirectionalLight3D as key light.
- WorldEnvironment ambient from sky, with a cool blue tint.
- Slightly higher saturation and slightly lower contrast in color
  adjustments. AgX tonemapping.
(Roblox Ambient/OutdoorAmbient map to Godot ambient light and sky.)

### 8. Wind (not in the tutorial, add as a card)
Vertex shader sway in Godot, weighted by height or vertex color so the
trunk base stays still. Parameters: wind_strength, wind_speed.

### 9. Distance (not in the tutorial, add as a card)
LOD levels and impostors for far trees. Use instancing (MultiMesh) for
forests.

## Related, for a future rock generator
- Procedural rocks: cube -> subdivision -> displace with Voronoi texture
  -> decimate -> merge vertices at center on uneven faces for flat facets
  -> shade smooth with auto smooth.
- Cavity/edge highlights: lighter color along convex edges, generated
  by script (e.g. curvature baked to vertex color), not painted by hand.
  Also applicable to tree bark and wooden props.
