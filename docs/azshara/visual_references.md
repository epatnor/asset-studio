# Visual references

Notes on the inspiration images in `patriks_input/` (screens from WoW:
Forever and classic WoW). They are references for mood, light and water,
not a style to copy. See CLAUDE.md, Art direction.

## What makes them work

- Atmosphere does most of the work. Thick, coloured fog and haze layer the
  scene into fore-, middle- and background; distant trees become flat
  silhouettes. Fog colour follows the sun: warm pink-orange at sunset,
  cold blue-teal in the dark forest, grey-white under moonlight.
- Light shafts (god rays) through the canopy and fog, and a big soft sun or
  moon disc with a wide glow. Strong backlight against the viewer.
- Water is dark and reflective, not turquoise. It mirrors the sky, the trees
  and the far shore, and the sun or moon draws a broken path of bright
  glints across it. Shallow edges show the bottom faintly. Shore foam is a
  thin, lacy line, not a band.
- Comparison shot (vanilla against Forever): the old water is flat bright
  blue; the new one is darker, takes its colour from the environment and
  the light, and shows the sun on the ripples.
- Ground has a lot of small detail close to the camera: grass tufts, reeds,
  flowers, lily pads, small rocks. Mid-distance is calmer.
- Paths are cobbled or worn dirt, clearly readable and leading the eye into
  the scene, often towards a light source.
- Dark zones: near-black silhouettes, twisted dead trees, low ground fog
  glowing teal, warm spots of torchlight. Colour comes from the light.
- Lush zones: warm low sun, deep greens in shadow, yellow-green where the
  light hits, accents of red foliage.

## What we can do on our budget

- Fog: Godot's depth fog plus height fog, colour per zone and per time of
  day, and a sun-scatter term so the fog glows towards the sun. Volumetric
  fog is not available in the Compatibility renderer; fake ground fog with
  large soft cards or a fog plane shader where needed.
- Light shafts: additive, soft-edged cards in the canopy, faded by angle to
  the sun. Cheap and art-directable.
- Sun and moon: a sky shader with a large soft disc and glow.
- Water: darker base colour, stronger sky reflection with the gradient and
  later a low-res sky cubemap, a sun-path glint band that follows the sun
  direction, thinner lacy foam.
- Close-up detail: grass, reeds, flowers and pebbles as MultiMesh (step 6).

## Patrik's own concept (81e83207..._skapad_av_patrik.png)

Target mood for the main island's lake valley:
- A lake nestled in a valley between soft cliffs overgrown with green; ruins
  on a far crag.
- Lush, dense grass with patches of small white and yellow flowers.
- Big, softly faceted dark boulders half sunk in the grass.
- Trees with round, full crowns and thick, slightly twisted trunks.
- Warm, low backlight with a sun glow and soft clouds; distant layers fade
  into haze.

## Patrik's GPT Image 2 sheets (ChatGPT-bild 12 okt. 2026 *.png)

Asset style targets and workflow guides, generated by Patrik:
- Trees: autumn trees with round, clumpy crowns built from many small
  leaf-cluster lumps (yellow, orange, red); a dead twisted tree; a dense
  round bush; dark faceted boulders.
- Ground: lush grass clumps, flower clumps (yellow, pink, white), a shore
  diorama with turquoise shallows and a mossy cliff.
- Ruins: broken columns, an ivy-covered arch, a crumbling wall with moss.
- Texture tiles: grass with daisies, dirt with pebbles, rock with moss,
  shallow water, sand with ripples, bark, leaves, sea foam water.
- Workflow sheets: trees (blockout, foliage clumps, wireframe, UV and
  texture, result), grass/flower cards with an atlas (RGBA, layered cards,
  vertex colour gradient), rocks and cliffs (blockout, shapes, low-poly
  wireframe, UV/texture, result), and the full pipeline mesh, UV, texture,
  vertex colour, shader, engine.
Take from them: crowns as clumps of lumps (we have ball crowns with tufts),
grass and flowers as textured cards in an atlas, faceted low-poly rocks with
a painted texture and moss on top, ruins for the dark island.

## Valheim (patriks_input/valheim_inspiration/)

Patrik's point: simple geometry and crude, low-resolution textures (blocky,
almost pixelated up close), yet the scenes are stunning. The light does
the work, not the assets. Not a style to copy, but proof that light and
atmosphere can carry a cheap scene.

What the light does:
- Fog is dense and close. Forests dissolve into haze within a few dozen
  metres, so every scene splits into a dark foreground, a hazy middle and
  a bright sky, and trunks read as flat silhouettes in layers.
- The haze takes its colour from the sun direction: warm orange-pink
  towards the sun, cold teal-blue away from it, often both in one frame
  (left half orange, right half blue). The sun glows through the fog and
  blooms into the trees around it.
- Shadows and ambient light are saturated blue-teal, never neutral grey.
  Warm sun against cool shade is the core contrast.
- Strong glow and bloom: the sky, the sun, sunlit fog and fires all bleed
  softly into their surroundings. Lens flares and soft bokeh discs in the
  sky.
- Point lights are small and intensely saturated (orange-pink fire) in an
  otherwise blue scene, so they pop as the only warm colour.
- Air is full of drifting motes (dust, pollen, snow) catching the light.
- Light shafts slant through the canopy, visible in the haze.
- Water is a glossy mirror of the sky and the fog colour, with a hard sun
  glint; reeds and rocks stand dark against it.
- Night is blue and readable, not black: lifted, coloured blacks.
- The grade is filmic and saturated, slightly lifted blacks, a bit of
  vignetting and chromatic aberration at the edges.

What we can do in the Compatibility renderer:
- Fog: much denser depth fog in the forest, a cool blue fog colour and a
  strong sun-scatter term, so the fog turns warm only towards the sun.
  Fog density can follow the zone and whether the camera is under canopy.
- Ambient: a saturated blue-teal ambient colour against a warm sun.
- Glow: lower threshold, more intensity and bloom, so sky, sunlit fog and
  lanterns bleed.
- Canopy shade: darken and cool the ground under tree crowns in the
  terrain shader (a canopy mask from the scatter), since there is no real
  ambient occlusion.
- Light shafts: additive soft cards in gaps of the canopy, faded by the
  angle to the sun.
- Motes: a cheap CPUParticles3D volume around the camera, small additive
  quads that glint when lit from behind.
- Fires and lanterns: small, very saturated warm lights with a glow sprite.
- Vignette and grading in a final post pass if the built-in adjustments
  are not enough.
- Textures can stay coarse (256-512 px); the effort goes into light.
